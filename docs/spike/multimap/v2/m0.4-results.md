2026-10-14, with the pre-registered settings: gate 8, beam 1 and 3, 200-pop budget, depth 6,
random seeds 1 to 5. Nothing was changed after the run (reading rule 5).

Command (paths shown as placeholders):

```
cargo run --release -p lci-maps --example walk_eval -- --scip <frozen index> \
  --gold <gold>/flow.json --root <tree at bfaff44> --out <result.json> \
  --gate 8 --seed 1 --pops 200 --max-depth 6
```

## Reading (rules 1 to 4)

1. **hit@1:** `rules` orders better than random in every group, including the no-shortcut and
   headline no-shortcut groups (0.510 and 0.529 against random means near 0.41). `rules-noname`
   passes on the headline no-shortcut group and on all items. On the no-shortcut group its
   interval reaches below the random mean, so that is no evidence.
2. **hit@3:** `rules` passes on all items and on the headline items. On the no-shortcut groups,
   neither arm leaves the random band, so that is no evidence.
3. **Name overlap (rule 2):** on the shortcut-flagged items, `rules` beats `rules-noname` by
   0.33 at hit@1. On the items without the flag the two are within each other's intervals
   (0.510 against 0.477, and 0.529 against 0.529). Most of the headline lift comes from shared
   names on the shortcut items, which is not evidence about structure.
4. **Path recall:** BFS reaches 97-100% of the gold paths inside the 200-pop budget. At this
   graph size and budget, path recall cannot tell ordering arms apart. No ordering arm separates
   from random on the no-shortcut items.

## Risk 2 signal (brief, M0 early signals)

Partly realised. Away from name shortcuts, the structural rules beat random only at hit@1,
by about +0.10, and not at hit@3 or on path recall [M]. On a graph of about 1,000 nodes, an
exhaustive closure within 200 pops already covers the paths, so ordering is not the bottleneck
at this scale [I]. Following the brief, M1 reports the walk as a size cap plus exact closures,
with the name signal reported separately as a shortcut. Whether ordering matters on the larger
A/B repositories is open; that needs a new pre-registration on a larger index, with a tighter
budget [I].
